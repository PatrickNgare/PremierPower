Testing and commissioning is the stage between "installed" and "handed over". It is where faults that would otherwise surface months later — as tripping, overheating or, at worst, a shock or a fire — are found while the walls are still easy to open. This article explains what happens at that stage and what a building owner should expect to receive.

## Inspection comes first

Before any instrument is connected, the installation is inspected with the power off. The inspector checks that the work matches the design: correct cable sizes and types, protective devices of the right rating, proper terminations, labelling of circuits and distribution boards, adequate support and segregation of cables, and that nothing is damaged. Many problems are found by eye at this stage.

## The core tests

The exact sequence follows the wiring standard the installation was designed to, but an initial verification of a new installation typically covers:

- **Continuity of protective conductors** — confirms that the earth path from every outlet, light and appliance back to the main earthing terminal is complete and of low resistance. A broken earth leaves metalwork able to become live without anything tripping.
- **Continuity of ring final circuits**, where rings are used — confirms the ring is complete and not broken or cross-connected.
- **Insulation resistance** — a high test voltage is applied between conductors and to earth to confirm the insulation is sound and nothing is damaged or wet.
- **Polarity** — confirms that switches and protective devices interrupt the line conductor, not the neutral, so a switched-off circuit is actually dead.
- **Earth electrode resistance** — measures how well the installation's earth electrode connects to the ground.
- **Earth fault loop impedance** — confirms that, if a fault to earth occurs, enough current will flow to operate the protective device quickly enough.
- **Residual current device (RCD) operation** — confirms each RCD trips within its required time at its rated current.
- **Functional checks** — switches, isolators, interlocks and controls are operated to confirm they do what they should.

Each result is recorded against the circuit it belongs to.

## Commissioning larger systems

On commercial and industrial projects, commissioning extends beyond the fixed wiring to the systems connected to it: main and sub-main distribution, standby generators and changeover, UPS systems, power factor correction, solar inverters, lighting controls and building management interfaces. These are energised in a planned order and their settings — protection relays, changeover timing, inverter limits — are set and verified under load.

## What you should receive

At handover, the building owner should have:

- a **test certificate** with the recorded results, signed by the licensed contractor responsible for the work;
- **as-built drawings** showing where the circuits actually run;
- **distribution board schedules** identifying every circuit;
- **manuals and settings** for any equipment commissioned — generators, UPS, inverters, controls.

Keep these together. Every future alteration, inspection or fault-finding visit starts from them.

## When testing should be repeated

Testing is not only for new work. Installations deteriorate, and buildings change use. Periodic inspection and testing — at intervals that depend on the type of building and how hard it is used — catches that deterioration before it becomes a fault. Commercial and industrial premises, and anywhere open to the public, generally need it more often than homes.
